    <h1>
      <em>Colophon.</em>
    </h1>
    <p class="lead">
      Notebooks have always had colophons &mdash; a small note at the back,
      describing the paper, the press, the type. Here's mine. Built in HTML
      and CSS, by hand, over more evenings than I'll admit.
    </p>
  </section>

  <section class="entry">
    <h2>↬ type.</h2>
    <p>
      Three faces, each doing exactly one job. <em>Mixing fewer than three felt
      thin; more than three felt loud.</em>
    </p>
    <div class="specimen serif">
      <span class="name">display + body</span>
      <span class="sample"><em>Instrument Serif</em></span>
    </div>
    <div class="specimen hand">

      <span class="sample">Caveat &mdash; pen ink</span>
    </div>
    <div class="specimen mono">
      <span class="name">technical</span>
      <span class="sample">Geist Mono · for code, labels, stamps</span>
    </div>
    <p style="margin-top: 20px;">
      All three are open-source and served via Google Fonts. The serif does the
      thinking; the handwriting does the talking; the mono does the receipts.
    </p>
  </section>

    <h2>↬ palette.</h2>
    <p>

      Dark mode is a <em>"library at midnight"</em> &mdash; deep warm black,
      glowing cream, jewel-tone Post-its. Not generic dark gray.
    </p>
  </section>

  <section class="entry">
    <h2>↬ principles.</h2>
    <ol class="principles">
      <li><b>Voice first.</b> The text was written before the layout. The layout serves the text.</li>
      <li><b>Asymmetry over symmetry.</b> Marginalia, tape strips, tilted cards. <em>Slight imperfection reads as care, not carelessness.</em></li>
      <li><b>One signal color.</b> Red appears when something matters. The rest of the page is calm.</li>
      <li><b>Hand-set anywhere a sentence can carry it.</b> The Caveat font is doing the work of three illustrations.</li>
      <li><b>Light mode <em>is</em> the design.</b> Dark mode is reframed, not inverted. Many sites get this wrong.</li>
      <li><b>No emoji, no stock icons, no AI-generated illustrations.</b> Either the system has a place for an element, or it doesn't appear.</li>
      <li><b>Every page has a footer.</b> Footers are where good websites tell you they're done. <em>This one is, almost.</em></li>
    </ol>
  </section>
